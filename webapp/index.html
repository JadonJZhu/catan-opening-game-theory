Opening Draft Explorer

Where should two players put their first settlements? The opening on a fixed 19-hex board, solved as a two-player zero-sum game.

How to read this

The moves. Every corner where a settlement could go is sorted into one of four categories: City (ore and grain), Road (wood and brick), Balanced, or Port. On each turn a player names a category and gets the best corner still open in it (open means not taken and not next to a taken corner). The draft is a snake: P1, P2, P2, P1.

The value of a hand. V = scarcity-weighted income (expected cards per 36 rolls, with rare resources counting more) + δ for each different resource + σ × the extra cards a port earns over trading 4:1 with the bank. σ = 1 means every card produced gets traded, so it is an upper bound on what a port is worth.

Zero-sum. Only one player wins, so the payoff is the lead: P1 gets V(P1) − V(P2) and P2 gets the negative. A gain for your opponent is a loss for you, which is why a player will sometimes take a spot mainly to keep it away from the other.

Why every Nash equilibrium gives the same value. In a finite two-player zero-sum game the minimax theorem says P1's best guaranteed lead (maximin) equals the lowest lead P2 can hold P1 to (minimax). That number is the value of the game, and every Nash equilibrium pays exactly it. Different equilibria can take different paths, but never to a different score.

Why we report the subgame-perfect one. Some equilibria rely on threats a player would not carry out once a mistake happens. The subgame-perfect equilibrium, found by backward induction, is optimal at every point of the draft, including after errors, so it is credible and tells you what to do from any position.

Checking it by hand. The reply matrix lists P1's lead for every opening and first reply, assuming the last two picks are played well. P2 picks the smallest number in the row (bold); P1 picks the row whose smallest number is largest (highlighted). That cell is the value of the game. Then try to beat the computer in Play the draft.
